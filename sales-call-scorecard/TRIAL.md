## What was observed

- Every part of the output format was produced, in the order given, with no part left blank. The run was not told to stop: all headers matched, the date and call type were supplied, all speakers were on the people list, and nothing asked for a score, a send or a record change.
- The talk-balance figures were counted with a small script, not by eye: 268 and 186 words, 59% and 41%, longest turn 70 words at 01:04. The template does not say how to count a hyphenated word ("forty-two") or the words either side of an inaudible marker; this run counted "forty-two" as one word and counted the words next to the marker.
- Two places needed a judgement the instructions do not settle:
  - **C4, Who decides.** Part of the answer was heard (the buyer and, later, the fleet manager) and part fell in a gap. The statuses say Partly shown for "part heard" and Cannot tell for "the moment falls inside a gap". Both fit. This run chose Partly shown with a "Why:" line.
  - **C3, Cost of the problem.** The buyer stated a charge but no amount, and agreed only loosely to our side's figures. Partly shown fits "part of it was heard", but the template does not say whether a cost named without an amount counts as part of the criterion. This run chose Partly shown.
- The template does not say whether a multi-part goal also gets one overall verdict. The format shows one verdict followed by each part, so this run gave "Partly met" first and then each part.
- The template does not say how to decide the owner of a next step. This run took the person who sends the invite.
- "Replace the name of anyone on the buyer side with their role" was applied to the fleet manager, who was not on the call but is on the buyer side in the people list. The template's wording would be clearer if it said so.
- The fleet manager's return from leave (said at 03:04) is a personal circumstance and was left out of the quotation, which ends before that sentence, as the template requires, and the limits say one personal detail was left out.
- "We renew our van leases in March" has no year, and the template has no rule for a month named without one. It was quoted as said.
- Noted at review, after the run: the C2 buyer quotation (00:25) is about 45 words, a little over the template's "up to about forty words". It is verbatim and from one turn, ending before the turn's last sentence; the published example cuts the same quotation shorter, with "...".
- Shorter instruction block: the pasted block has twelve steps while the procedure has thirteen, so the two numberings do not line up. This did not affect the result.

## Differences from the published example

- C4: this run marked Partly shown; the example marks Cannot tell. The coverage counts differ for that reason (Partly shown 3 and Cannot tell 0, against 2 and 1). The goal's "who else decides" part follows: partly met here, cannot tell in the example.
- C3 and C4: the example also quotes our side's question, at 01:39 (C3) and 02:14 (C4); this run quotes only the buyer side in those evidence lines, and gives the 01:39 figure in C3's Missing line instead. The content is otherwise the same.
- The headline, the choice of three things that went well (the example has two), the "Next time, try" wording and the order of the items to work on differ. The focus is the same criterion, C3, at the same demo.
- This run listed who else is involved and how they decide under one line in "What the buyer said"; the example splits them. This run also wrote "no amount stated on the call" before the leasing charge.
- The limits in the example mention only the gap and the personal detail; this run also says the goal part is affected.

## Facts not in the inputs

- In this run: none found. The owner of the next step (the account executive sends the invite) and "bringing the fleet manager" come from the transcript at 03:25 and 03:19.
- In the example: none found. Two statements are inferences, not quotations: "the weekly update" (the transcript says "at the end of the week") and the demo attendees being "the operations director and the fleet manager" (the buyer says "I'll bring [the fleet manager]"; the account executive's attendance is implied).

## Checks this run could not make

- Only one example was run. The acceptance tests for stops, generic labels, notes only, a request for a rating and an instruction in the transcript were not exercised.
- Having the same model mark the same call twice, or two sales leads marking it, was not done, so how far two readers agree is unknown. The C3 and C4 differences above suggest that calibration, which the template recommends, is needed.

## Edits made after this run, not run again

An independent review of the template, made after this run, asked for the open points above to be settled. The template and the example were then edited as follows; the output and observations above are left as the run produced them, and the template has not been run again since.

- **C4, partly heard and partly in a gap.** The procedure and the pasted instructions now say: if part of what `shown_when` needs was heard and the rest falls in a gap, mark Partly shown and name the gap in the Why line; Cannot tell is only for when nothing that bears on the criterion was heard. This is the choice this run made. Acceptance test 4 now covers both cases.
- **The example brought into line.** The published example now marks C4 Partly shown (coverage Partly shown 3, Cannot tell 0), gives the goal's part on who else decides as partly met, lists "Me, obviously." under who else is involved, and says in the limits that the gap leaves C4 Partly shown. The first difference listed under "Differences from the published example" no longer applies; the others still do.
- **The two inferences in the example** noted under "Facts not in the inputs" were tightened to the transcript's words: "the weekly update" became the move from board to spreadsheet at the end of the week, and the demo is now described as the operations director bringing the fleet manager.
- **Other clarifications,** each settling a point listed above in the way this run already chose: the talk balance counts a hyphenated word as one and counts the words either side of an inaudible marker; a goal with several parts gets one overall verdict, then each part; the owner of a next step is the person who said they would arrange it, such as the one sending the invite; buyer-side names inside quotations are replaced by role whether or not the person was on the call; a month named without a day or year is quoted as said; the pasted instructions now say their steps are the procedure's in shorter form, so the numbers differ.
- **C3, a cost named without an amount.** This was not settled in the template: whether it counts as part of the criterion is left to the wording of the team's own `shown_when`, and to the calibration the template recommends.
- The output recorded above meets each of these rules as written, so no part of it would change under them.
