# Deal close plan: tried once on its invented example

- **Product:** Claude, run in Claude Code
- **Model:** Claude Sonnet 5.5 (model ID claude-sonnet-5-5)
- **Date:** 2026-11-10

This was one run of the workflow template on its own invented example. The inputs are the invented ones in [the example](example.md); no client data was used. The run was made by an AI assistant that did not write the template. It was given the template's instruction block and output format and the example's five inputs (deal facts, deal record row, quote, buyer notes, approval steps), and it did not look at the example's output until its own was finished. Nothing was sent, shared or recorded. This is a record of one run, not a test in a client environment.

## What was observed

- **Every required part was produced:** the header, all six business case lines, the nine-step plan with planned and must-finish-by dates, Part 2 in full, the covering note and the source key. Nothing in the instructions made the run stop; the inputs were complete, the headers matched, and there was one deal.
- **Against the published example:** the dates, spare days, chain, target message (9 working days late), sums (seven months to recover, $9,000 a month, Monday 18 January 2027 and Friday 22 January 2027) and record proposals agree. The differences were these.
  - The example puts the $6,000 a month penalties in line 1 of Part 1. This run held them out of Part 1 and listed them under "Not used in the business case", because the procedure and the instruction block say a cost with no target stays out of Part 1. The checklist's fourth acceptance test says such a cost is "shown under the problem today", which contradicts that. The template should say which.
  - The example names step 3 "Approve quarterly billing", which tells the buyer why the step exists. The template says internal steps show "never the reason or the threshold behind them". This run used a neutral name, "Internal approval before the order form goes out". The example's wording reads as a breach of the template's own rule.
  - Wording of step names and the covering note differs; the content is the same.
- **Where the instructions were ambiguous and a guess was needed:**
  - Which done events count as steps. The discovery call, the demo and the sent quote are not listed as steps; only the security answers (named as a pending dependency) are. The template does not say whether earlier milestones belong in the plan.
  - Whether approval step "billing other than annually in advance" applies. The quote bills annually; only a buyer email says procurement will ask for quarterly billing. This run included it and flagged why. The template says to check the condition "against the quote and the notes" but not what to do with a request that has not yet been made.
  - When step 3 is planned. The inputs do not say when procurement will ask, so it waits on nothing and lands on Thursday 19 November. A reader could argue it should wait on procurement's request, which comes after the CFO meeting.
  - Whether a promise to the buyer, made in the deal facts, is "date agreed". This run said yes, since the owner is the account executive and the date is in the deal facts. The buyer has not agreed it.
  - "Our lawyers" carries no role. This run followed acceptance test 10 and wrote "to be named by the buyer"; the template does not say whether a group with no named role counts as named.
  - The CFO meeting needs an owner on each side, but no input says who from the seller attends. This run did not invent one and flagged it.
  - The $18,000 is "about" and the $6,000 is "something like"; the template has no rule for approximate figures, so they were carried as the buyer said them.
  - "Cut in half" was read as a $9,000 a month gain; the template does not say how to turn a proportion into an amount.
  - Whether the countersignature belongs in step 9 or as its own step. This run merged it, as the output format's "with [role]" suggests.
  - The template's customize list asks for "[your default step length]" but it was never used here, because every step had a stated length.
- **Anything the template told it to stop for:** none.
- **Facts in the outputs that are not in the inputs.** Neither output adds a fact. Both state derived values (weekdays, 31 March 2027 as the end of March, the 42-day count, $9,000 and $108,000). Both name step wording of their own, such as "CFO reviews the numbers and decides the budget", which the buyer's email implies but does not say in those words.
- **Shortcomings of the workflow template seen in this run:** the output is long and has many moving parts, so a person must check every date; the template says so itself ("Check every date and every sum"). One run on one example shows it can follow the format; it does not show it works on a real deal or in other AI products.

## Changes made after this run

These were made on 10 November 2026, after the run above, and have not been run again. The output and observations above are left as the run produced them. Each change settles a point the run found open, in the way the run had already handled it.

- A buyer cost with no target now stays out of Part 1 entirely, as the procedure and the instruction block already said. The fourth acceptance test was corrected to match, and the example's $6,000 a month penalties were taken out of line 1 of its business case; they stay under "Not used in the business case".
- An internal step is now named in Part 1 only as an internal approval and the step it comes before, never by what it approves; what it approves and why it applies go in Part 2. The example's step 3 was renamed "Internal approval before the order form goes out", the name this run used, and its "Internal lines" entry now says what the step approves.
- New rules, in the procedure and the instruction block: an approval step whose condition the quote does not meet, but which a buyer note says the buyer will ask for, is included and flagged "condition expected, not yet met", and waits on nothing unless the inputs say when the request comes; a done step is listed only when a step still to happen waits on it, so earlier calls, demos and the sent quote are not steps; a date the seller promised on its own step is "date agreed", a buyer step's date only when a note shows its owner agreed it; a department named as doing a step is the owner as named, and a group that does not say who acts, such as "our lawyers", is "to be named by the buyer"; a buyer meeting with no attendee from the seller's side stated gets no second owner and the flag "your side's attendee not stated"; an approximate figure keeps the buyer's own word where it is first stated, and the sums use the figure as given; a goal stated as a share of a cost becomes an amount by applying the share to the cost; the countersignature is part of the signatures step unless the approval steps give it a later day.
- The example now carries the step 3 and step 5 flags this run raised, the buyer's words "our lawyers" beside step 7, weekdays on the dates in its header and source key, and the year on the dates in Part 2. Acceptance test 16 was added for an approval whose condition is expected but not yet met, and the output format's "From:" line and source key now ask for weekdays. The instruction block also gained one sentence on how a missing deal record, quote, buyer notes or approval steps is written, which the procedure already said; this run had all five inputs.
- Read against the revised rules, the output above follows them in substance, with these differences of form: its step 3 and step 5 flags are worded in its own words rather than "condition expected, not yet met" and "your side's attendee not stated"; its "Internal lines" entry gives only the step number, with what step 3 approves stated under Flags instead; and its source key has no weekdays.

## Changes made after the independent review

These were also made on 10 November 2026, after the independent review of the template, and have not been run either. The output and observations above are still left as the run produced them.

- The sixth business case line is now called "What they valued", in the procedure, the instruction block, the output format, the example and the public page. The output above uses the earlier label, "Why us".
- Procedure steps 12 and 13 were swapped, so the check for usual steps nobody mentioned comes before Part 2 is written. The instruction block already folded the two together and is unchanged on this point.
- A part of the quote linked to a goal that no input itself ties it to is now flagged in Part 2, "link not stated by the buyer", in the procedure, the instruction block and the output format's Flags line. The example now flags its line 3 link from the expiry alerts to halving write-offs, since no note says the alerts will do that. The output above makes the same link without flagging it; under the revised rules that is a missing flag, not only a difference of form.
