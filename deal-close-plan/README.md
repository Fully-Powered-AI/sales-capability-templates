# Deal close plan

Your account executive holds one late-stage deal on two pages: the buyer's case for buying now, counted from the buyer's own figures, and a dated plan of every step to signature with its owner on each side. The seller decides what the buyer sees; nothing reaches the sales system without approval.

*Capability template · Sales · version 0.1.0 · [Capabilities Directory](https://fullypowered.ai/capabilities/deal-close-plan/)*

## The outcome

The deal stops depending on hope and a close date somebody typed in weeks ago. Your account executive walks into the last stretch with the buyer's own argument for buying, in the buyer's own words: what the problem costs them today, what they want to achieve, what each part of your offer does toward it, and plain arithmetic weighing the price against their own numbers. Nothing in it is a benchmark or a borrowed result, so the buyer's champion can carry it into the room with the person who signs and defend every line.

Beside it sits the plan both sides can work to. Every step still to happen before signature is on it: the budget meeting, the security review, the review of your order form, supplier setup, your own approvals and the signatures, each with an owner and a date. The plan shows which steps actually set the signature date and how much room every other step has. If the date in your sales system cannot be reached, the plan says so plainly and shows the earliest date that can, so the forecast conversation starts from facts.

## Who starts the work, and when

The account executive responsible for the deal starts it, or the founder or partner doing the selling. The right moment is when the buyer has asked for a quote or proposal and the paperwork is about to begin. Run it again each time the path shifts: a new date, a new owner, an extra step on the buyer's side.

The account executive reads the plan and decides what the buyer sees, whether to send the covering note, and which proposed changes to make in the sales system. Prices and exceptions stay with the people your company names to approve them. The AI assistant sends nothing, shares nothing and changes no record.

## What they supply

- **The buyer's own words.** Call notes, transcripts and emails, each with its date and who was speaking.
- **The quote as the buyer received it.** Prices, discounts, one-time fees, the term, billing and how long setup takes.
- **The deal record.** The deal's single row, saved out of your sales system or spreadsheet: stage, amount, close date and next step.
- **Your deal facts.** Today's date, the target signature date if it is not the record's, who is on the deal from your side, closed days, and anything already promised to the buyer.
- **Your approval steps.** The internal sign-offs your company needs, when each one is needed, who signs it off and how long that takes.

## What comes out

One plan in two parts.

1. **The draft for the buyer.** The business case in six lines (the problem today, what they want, what the offer does, the arithmetic, the cost of waiting, what they valued) and the mutual action plan, step by step, with owners, planned dates and the date each step must finish by. Every line carries a tag to its source, removed before sharing.
2. **Notes for you alone.** Whether the target date holds, the chain of steps that sets the signature date, the spare days on every other step, the owners and dates still to confirm with the buyer, the points held out of the business case until the buyer backs them, usual steps nobody has mentioned yet, the changes proposed for your sales record, and a short covering note to the buyer's main contact, ready for you to edit and send.

A good close plan is one your champion forwards to their boss without editing, and one your sales lead can check line by line against the notes.

## What has to change for your company

- **Your approval steps.** Write down each internal sign-off, the deals it covers, the person who signs it off and its length in working days. The plan is only as accurate as this list.
- **Your record's columns.** Agree the export's headers once; if your sales system names them differently, write the mapping down, and the assistant will stop and ask rather than guess.
- **A habit of the buyer's words.** Dated notes that capture what the buyer actually said, figures included, are what turn the business case from a sales pitch into their own argument.
- **The default length of a step** nobody has given a length for, and the extra stages your buyers tend to have, a board vote or a pilot sign-off, for example.
- **Who sees internal steps.** Decide whether your own approvals show up in what the buyer reads.
- **Your platform.** The instructions are written to be pasted into ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini. It works from files and notes you supply and needs no connection to your sales system.
