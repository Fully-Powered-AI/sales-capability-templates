# Cold outreach sequence drafting: an invented example

*Illustrative example with invented data.* The company, the prospect, the people, the job posting and every fact below are invented. Firm N is an invented six-person bookkeeping firm; Prospect P is an invented 45-person heating and cooling contractor. First come the inputs the founder supplied, in full, then the sequence the capability returned.

## The inputs

```
YOUR FACTS (supplied by the sender)

Who we are: Firm N, a six-person bookkeeping firm.
What we do, and for whom: We keep the books for specialty trade
  contractors with 20 to 100 employees: job costing, payables and the
  month-end close.
Proof we can support:
  1. Every client's month closes on a written checklist the client can read.
  2. We work inside the accounting software the client already uses.
     Nothing is migrated.
  3. Our founder ran the back office of an electrical contractor for nine
     years before starting Firm N.
What we never say:
  - No client names. No client has given permission.
  - No figures for time or money saved. We have not measured any.
  - No prices in outreach.
Something to read: "The 14-point month-end checklist for trade
  contractors", two pages. It exists and is current.
Sender: Dana, founder. Signs as "Dana".

THE PROSPECT

Company: Prospect P, a 45-person heating and cooling contractor, one office.
Person: the operations manager, first name Lee.
Address source: Lee's work address is on the company's "Our team" page.
Fact 1: Job posting, "Accounts payable and job-cost clerk", on the
  company's careers page, posted 26 October 2026. It says: "You will help
  us get month-end close under ten working days" and "reconcile supplier
  invoices to job numbers".
Fact 2: Company news page, 2 September 2026: the company opened a second
  service territory.
Leave out: anything from the owner's personal social posts. Do not name
  the new territory.

THE ASK
Note 1: permission to send the checklist. Later: a 20-minute call.

SEQUENCE SETTINGS
Four notes, on days 0, 4, 10 and 21.

VOICE SAMPLE (an email Dana sent to a supplier)
Hi Sam,
Quick one. The October statement shows two invoices we paid on the 3rd.
Can you check your side and send a corrected statement?
Thanks,
Dana
```

## The output

**Outreach sequence: Prospect P, operations manager**

Status: Drafts for Dana. Not sent. Dana decides whether, to whom and when, and sends each note herself.

**Angle**

- **Level:** company. Two dated facts were supplied.
- **Facts used:** Fact 1, the job posting of 26 October 2026, both quotations.
- **Facts not used:** Fact 2, the second territory. It adds nothing to the ask, and the leave-out line limits it.
- **Guess, stated nowhere as fact:** a company hiring a clerk to shorten its close probably has the close on its mind now, and matching invoices to jobs may be where the days go. It appears only as the question in Note 2.
- **Shape:** something small to read first. The checklist exists and matches the goal the posting states.
- **Voice:** from the supplier email. Short greeting, short sentences, a plain question, "Thanks," and the first name to sign off.
- **Defaults used:** the 120-word limit, since none was set. The later ask, a 20-minute call, was not given a note; proposed: Note 3. That left Note 2 without an ask; proposed: a reply to a question.

**Note 1, day 0**

```
Subject: month-end close

Hi Lee,

Your posting for an accounts payable and job-cost clerk says the goal is
month-end close in under ten working days.

We keep the books for trade contractors your size, inside the accounting
software you already use. Every month closes on a written checklist the
client can read.

I have a two-page version of that checklist for trade contractors.
Want me to send it?

Thanks,
Dana
[Firm N identification line]
[opt-out line]
```

**Note 2, day 4.** Adds: the second task in the posting. Use only if there has been no reply.

```
Subject: invoices and job numbers

Hi Lee,

The same posting mentions reconciling supplier invoices to job numbers.
Job costing and payables are the work we do for trade contractors.

Is that where the days go in your close, or is it somewhere else?

Thanks,
Dana
[Firm N identification line]
[opt-out line]
```

**Note 3, day 10.** Adds: a reason to believe. Use only if there has been no reply.

```
Subject: a contractor's back office

Hi Lee,

Before I started Firm N I ran the back office of an electrical contractor
for nine years. Firm N keeps the books for trade contractors with 20 to
100 people, in the software they already have.

Would 20 minutes on how we run a month-end close be worth it? Reply
with a time and I'll confirm.

Thanks,
Dana
[Firm N identification line]
[opt-out line]
```

**Note 4, day 21.** The close. Use only if there has been no reply.

```
Subject: month-end close, last note

Hi Lee,

This is my last note on this. If the checklist would ever help with your
month-end close, it is yours for the asking.

Good luck with the hire.

Thanks,
Dana
[Firm N identification line]
[opt-out line]
```

**What ends the sequence**

- Any reply from Lee, on any channel, or a booked call, ends the remaining notes.
- A yes to Note 1 gets the checklist and nothing else. It is not turned into a request for a call.
- "Not now" with a date: stop, and Dana notes the date.
- A request to be left alone: stop for good, unless Lee writes again.
- An automatic away message: pause until the return date. A bounce: stop; no other address is looked for.

**Grounding**

- "Your posting for an accounts payable and job-cost clerk says the goal is month-end close in under ten working days" (Note 1): Fact 1, the first quotation, paraphrased, 26 October 2026.
- "trade contractors your size" (Note 1): your facts, 20 to 100 employees; Prospect P has 45.
- "inside the accounting software you already use" (Note 1): proof 2.
- "closes on a written checklist the client can read" (Note 1): proof 1.
- "a two-page version of that checklist" (Note 1): something to read.
- "reconciling supplier invoices to job numbers" (Note 2): Fact 1, quoted.
- "Job costing and payables are the work we do" (Note 2): what we do.
- "ran the back office of an electrical contractor for nine years" (Note 3): proof 3.
- "Firm N keeps the books for trade contractors with 20 to 100 people" (Note 3): what we do, and for whom.
- "in the software they already have" (Note 3): proof 2.
- "If the checklist would ever help with your month-end close, it is yours for the asking" (Note 4): something to read.
- "Good luck with the hire" (Note 4): Fact 1, the open posting.

**Read before sending**

- Note 1 says "trade contractors your size". Your facts say 20 to 100 employees and the prospect entry says 45 people. Check the headcount is still right, or use the facts page's own words.
- Note 1 says the software "you already use". Proof 2 supports working in whatever the client has; nothing supplied says which software Prospect P uses, and the note does not name one.
- Note 2's question is the guess put as a question. It claims nothing about Prospect P's close.
- Notes 1 and 2 rest on the posting, and Note 4 assumes it is still open on day 21. Check the careers page on the morning each goes.
- No freshness limit was set. The oldest fact used is the posting of 26 October 2026.
- The asks in Notes 2 and 3 are proposed. The inputs gave Note 1's ask and "later: a call" without naming a note.

**Slots for Dana**

- The identification line and the opt-out line her recipients' country requires. The capability does not decide what the law requires.
- The mailbox each note goes from.
- A booking route for the call, if she wants one. None was supplied, so Note 3 asks for a reply.
- The checklist itself, sent only after a yes.

**Left out on purpose:** client names, any figure for time or money saved, prices, the second territory and the owner's personal posts.
