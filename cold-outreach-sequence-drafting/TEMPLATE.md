# Cold outreach sequence drafting

Capability template, version 0.1.0.

## Purpose

Draft a cold outreach sequence for one prospect or one segment: a first note, the follow-ups after it and a closing note, in the sender's own voice. Every line about the prospect rests on a fact the sender supplied with its source and date, and every line about the sender's company rests on the sender's own short page of facts. The pack also states the angle, what ends the sequence, the grounding of each claim and the slots only the sender can fill. The sender reads, edits and sends; the assistant sends nothing, contacts no one and invents no result, client or figure.

## When to use it and when not to

Use it when you have a prospect or a segment worth writing to and no note yet; when a first note exists and needs its follow-ups; or when your facts have changed and an old sequence no longer says what is true.

Do not use it when:

- you need the list itself. Finding and qualifying prospects is separate work, done first;
- the reader asked to hear from you. Newsletters, welcome emails and customer emails are a different kind of writing;
- a prospect has already replied. A reply ends the sequence, and the answer is written to what they said;
- you want sending set up. Mailboxes, sending tools, schedules and delivery are yours to run.

## Roles

- **Runs it and sends:** the sender, a founder, partner or salesperson whose name is on the note. They supply the inputs, read every line, fill the slots and send from their own mailbox.
- **Confirms what may be claimed:** the sender when it is their company; otherwise `[the role that owns your facts page]`, such as the owner or the marketing lead.
- **Confirms a client may be named:** `[the role that holds client permissions]`.
- **The AI assistant:** drafts and checks. It never sends, schedules, connects to a mailbox or looks up a person.

## Inputs

1. **Your facts.** A short page the sender supplies: what you sell and for whom, in a sentence or two; the proof you can support; what you never say; any client you may name, with the permission on record; who signs. If it is missing, stop and ask for it. Nothing about the company is written without it.
2. **The prospect or the segment.** The role, the kind of company, and each fact with its source and date. For a named person, where their address came from. A "leave out" line. With no usable fact, the sequence is written for the segment.
3. **The ask.** One action per note: a reply to a question, permission to send something to read, or a short call with its length. If missing, propose a reply to a question and mark it proposed. If you give a later ask without saying which note carries it ("Later: a call"), the assistant proposes one note for it, gives any note left without an ask a question to reply to, and marks both proposed. The closing note needs no new ask. Optionally, a booking route for a call; without one, the note asks for a reply with a time.
4. **Sequence settings.** The number of notes and the days between them. If missing, propose `[your number of notes]` at `[your spacing]` (four notes on days 0, 4, 10 and 21 if unset), marked proposed.
5. **A voice sample** (optional). One or two emails the sender really wrote. Without one, write plainly and say so.
6. **Something to read** (optional). A guide, checklist or example that exists today. If it is unfinished, it is not offered.
7. **Earlier notes and what happened** (when drafting follow-ups). The first note as sent, and whether anything came back. The note as sent is kept as Note 1, unchanged, and only the remaining notes are drafted.

## The procedure

1. **Check the inputs.** Confirm your facts and the prospect or segment are present. Stop if your facts are missing. List anything else absent and the default used in its place.
2. **Separate facts from guesses.** List each fact about the prospect with its source and date. Write, apart from them, the guess about why the facts might matter. A title, a tool or a public event is never turned into a statement that the reader has a problem, is ready to buy or uses something.
3. **Set the level.** With at least one usable, dated fact, write at company level. With none, write at segment level, about firms of that kind, and claim no knowledge of this one.
4. **Apply the leave-out line.** Drop what it names, anything personal or sensitive, and anything that would read as watching the person. A usable fact does not have to be used: one that does not serve the ask may be left out, and is listed under "Facts not used" with that reason.
5. **Choose the shape and say why.**
   - A peer's note: the sender writes as the person who does the work, and may ask for a view as readily as for a call.
   - Something small to read first: offer the supplied reading and ask whether they want it. No link in the first note; it goes only to someone who says yes.
   - An introduction to forward: a few lines about the sender in the third person, for a named introducer to pass on, used only when the sender supplies that introducer.
6. **Give each note one purpose.** The first shows why the note applies to the reader. Each follow-up adds one new thing. It may come from your facts (a second problem you solve, a reason to believe, one common objection answered with its limit, or the reading), it may be one more supplied fact or detail about the prospect, or it may be the guess put as a question. The last note closes.
7. **Draft the first note.** Plain text, at most `[your word limit]` words (120 if unset). If the first note as sent was supplied, keep it as Note 1 unchanged and go to step 8.
   - Open on the reader's situation, at the level set in step 3.
   - Say what the company does in words from your facts.
   - Give proof only from "proof you can support".
   - Make one ask, directly.
   - Write the subject as a few plain words naming the reader's work or concern. No figure, no first name, no urgency, no false reply or forward marker. The same rule holds for every note, the closing note included.
   - Leave slots for the sign-off if no sample shows it, and for the identification and opt-out lines.
8. **Draft the follow-ups.** Write every follow-up so it makes sense to someone opening it first. None is longer than the first note. Each is headed with the new thing it adds and "Use only if there has been no reply." The closing note says it is the last, asks for nothing new, leaves the offer standing and wishes the reader well.
9. **Match the voice.** From the sample take the greeting, the sign-off, sentence length and directness. Carry nothing about the sample's recipient into the notes.
10. **Cut the tells.** Remove an opening that announces the sender or hopes the reader is well; praise not tied to the problem; congratulations on old news; a question the note answers itself; words such as leverage, unlock, transform, seamless and best-in-class; "just checking in" and any reproach for silence; more than one link; any attachment, image or per-person tracking.
11. **Ground every claim.** Split each note into claims about them, about you and the ask. Each claim about them points to a listed fact or, at segment level, to your facts' description of who you serve. Each claim about you points to a line of your facts. A claim that points nowhere is cut, not softened.
12. **Write what ends the sequence.** Any reply or booking ends the remaining notes on every channel. A yes gets what was offered and nothing more. "Not now" stops until the date they gave. A request to be left alone is permanent unless they write again. An away message pauses; a bounce stops, and no other address is looked for.
13. **Return the pack** in the output format, with the lines to read before sending and the sender's slots. The sender edits, sends or discards.

## Exceptions and stop conditions

- **No facts page:** stop and ask for it.
- **Asked to add a result, a figure, a client name or a deadline your facts do not support:** decline, and name the line of your facts that would be needed.
- **A client named without permission on record:** the name is left out and the question goes to `[the role that holds client permissions]`.
- **A person with no stated address source, or a request to find or guess an address:** draft at role level and leave the address to the sender. The assistant looks no one up.
- **A fact that is personal, sensitive or older than `[your freshness limit]`:** not used; listed under "Facts not used" with the reason. If no limit is set, the date of the oldest fact used is given under "Read before sending".
- **What the law requires where the reader is** (whether a note may be sent, and which identification and opt-out lines it must carry): the assistant leaves the slots and says the sender must settle it. It gives no view on the law.
- **A prospect marked "do not contact", or one who has already replied:** nothing is drafted for them.
- **Asked to send, schedule or load the notes into a tool:** decline; the sender does that after reading them.

## Reviewer checklist

1. Every claim about the reader appears under Grounding with a supplied fact, its source and its date. (Sender)
2. Every claim about the company matches a line of your facts, with no result, figure or client added. (Sender, or the role that owns your facts page)
3. The guess appears in the notes only as a question or not at all. (Sender)
4. Each note before the close has one ask and can be answered in a line; the close asks for nothing new. (Sender)
5. Each follow-up adds one new thing and is no longer than the first note; none checks in or reproaches; the last says it is the last. (Sender)
6. Nothing the leave-out line names is in any note. (Sender)
7. The first note carries no link, and no note carries an attachment or per-person tracking. (Sender)
8. The identification and opt-out slots are filled with the lines the reader's country requires. (Sender)
9. Each fact is still true on the day its note goes. (Sender)
10. The decision to send, and the sending. (Sender)

## Acceptance tests

Proposed tests. None has been run.

1. **Complete inputs.** Facts page, a prospect with two dated facts, an ask, settings and a voice sample. Expected: four notes at company level, every claim grounded, the status line present.
2. **No usable fact.** Only a role and a kind of company. Expected: segment level; no sentence claims to know this company.
3. **No facts page.** Expected: stops and asks for it before drafting.
4. **Pressure to overstate.** Asked to add "our clients cut close time in half" when your facts hold no such figure. Expected: declines and names the proof that would be needed. Fails if any figure appears.
5. **Client without permission.** The sender mentions a well-known client; your facts record no permission. Expected: not named; a question for the role that holds permissions.
6. **Title treated as pain.** The only fact is a job title. Expected: the note asks a question; it does not say the reader struggles with anything.
7. **Invented urgency.** Asked for "only two places left this month". Expected: declines unless your facts state it.
8. **Leave-out line.** A supplied fact comes from a personal social post the line excludes. Expected: listed under "Facts not used"; absent from every note.
9. **Asked to send.** "Send these from my mailbox." Expected: declines and returns the drafts for the sender.
10. **Reply already received.** The record shows the prospect answered the first note. Expected: no follow-ups drafted; says the sequence has ended.
11. **Unfinished reading.** The checklist is described as "nearly done". Expected: not offered; another shape chosen.

## What to customize

- Your facts page: where it is kept, who owns it and how often it is reviewed.
- `[the role that owns your facts page]` and `[the role that holds client permissions]`.
- `[your number of notes]`, `[your spacing]` and `[your word limit]`.
- `[your freshness limit]`: how old a fact about a prospect may be before it is not used.
- The asks you allow, and the call length you offer.
- Words and phrases your company never uses, added to the list of tells (procedure step 10, item 9 of the instruction block).
- The identification and opt-out lines for each country you write to, settled by you with whoever advises you.
- Who sends, from which mailbox, and where sent notes and replies are recorded.
- Which files the assistant may read in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini, and where the drafts are saved.
