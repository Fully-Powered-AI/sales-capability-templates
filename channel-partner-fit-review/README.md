# Channel partner fit review

When a referral, reseller or alliance partner approaches, the founder or head of partnerships gets one review: evidence the partner brings customers of its own, the type that evidence supports, a ninety-day plan with signs to go on or stop, and the commission range that still beats selling direct. A person decides.

*Capability template · Sales · version 0.1.0 · [Capabilities Directory](https://fullypowered.ai/capabilities/channel-partner-fit-review/)*

## The outcome

A firm offers to sell your product, and the conversation quickly turns to margin, a region of its own and a date to sign. This review gives you the facts before you answer. Every customer the partner names is checked against your own records, so you can see which ones are genuinely new to you, and every claim is marked as confirmed, only stated by the partner, or unknown.

From that evidence the review shows which kind of partnership the facts actually support today: one that introduces buyers while you sell, one that sells and bills on its own, or one that brings a combined offering to market with you. If the partner asks for more than the evidence supports, the review says exactly what would close the gap.

Then it does the arithmetic with your own numbers. It shows the commission or margin below which a deal through the partner still earns more than the same deal won by your own team, both per deal and across a year at the volume the partner promises, and where the partner's ask sits against that range. Finally it sets out a ninety-day plan whose targets come straight from the partner's own promise and your win rate, with the signs that would show it is working and the signs that would end it.

You decide. The review proposes no rate, makes no counter-offer and contacts nobody.

## Who starts the work, and when

The founder, the head of partnerships or the business development lead runs it as soon as a firm asks to introduce, resell or sell alongside you, and before anyone promises a type, a rate or a region. It runs again when the partner sends new evidence or changes what it asks for.

The founder, or whoever owns partnerships, reads it and decides. Finance supplies the numbers once and refreshes them each quarter.

## What they supply

- **What the partner sent:** its application, email or presentation, with what it asks for, the customers it names and what it expects to sell.
- **Your notes from talking to the partner,** including any calls you made to customers it named.
- **An export of your accounts,** customers and opportunities with the date each first appeared, so the partner's customers can be checked against your records.
- **Your economics from finance:** average first-year deal value, cost of delivery, what it costs you to win a deal yourself, your own cost per partner deal and per year for each type, your win rate and your sales cycle.
- **Your partner programme,** if you have one, and a few facts: the review date, who decides, who handles agreements and what counts as a qualified opportunity.

## What comes out

One review in a fixed order:

1. **What the partner asks for,** in its own words.
2. **Evidence of its own customers,** each one checked against your records and marked by how far it is confirmed, with a count.
3. **Warning signs,** each backed by a fact, such as a rush to sign or a named customer you already had.
4. **The partner type that fits,** requirement by requirement, and what would change it.
5. **Economics against selling direct:** break-even rates per deal and per year, the partner's ask set against them, and the assumptions behind the figures.
6. **A ninety-day joint plan** for the type the evidence supports: the steps before day one, and the signs to watch at day 30, day 45 and day 90.
7. **Agreement terms** the partner raised, listed for the person who handles agreements.
8. **Questions before deciding,** each addressed to the person who can answer it, and a blank decision line.

A good review lets you answer the partner in one meeting, knowing what you would agree to and why.

## What has to change for your company

- **Get your numbers from finance.** The economics use only your own figures: the review never supplies a typical rate or cost. Agree them once and refresh them each quarter.
- **Decide your partner types,** if you have a programme, and what each requires. Without one, three plain types are used: introduce, resell, or sell a combined offering together.
- **Keep your accounts export current,** with the date each account first appeared. Its column names are set once; if yours differ, write down which is which, and the assistant stops and asks rather than guess.
- **Say what counts as a qualified opportunity,** because the ninety-day targets are counted in them.
- **Name the people:** who decides, who is your contact for the partner, and who handles agreements.
- **Your platform.** The instructions are written to be pasted into ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini. It works from files you supply and needs no connection to your sales or accounting systems.
