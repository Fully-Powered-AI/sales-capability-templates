# Channel partner fit review: tried on its invented example

- **Product:** Claude, run in Claude Code
- **Model:** Claude Opus 5.5 (model ID `claude-opus-5-5`)
- **Date:** 2026-11-10
- **Template version:** 0.1.0

This is a record of runs of the capability template on its own invented example. The seller, Partner A, its customers, the people, the figures and the dates are all invented, and no client data was used. It shows what the template produced on one set of inputs. It is not a test in any company's environment.

This is the third run on the same example, all on 10 November 2026. The first run, by a different model that did not write the template, showed where the instructions left room to guess. After an independent review the template and the example were revised, and a second run was made on the revised instructions by the model that made the revision. A second independent review then raised eight minor points, five of which the second run had itself listed as places where a reader could still have to guess. The procedure, the pasted instructions, the output format and the example's output were revised again in small ways, and the run recorded here was made on those instructions. What the second run observed, what the first run found, and what was changed after each, is at the end.

## The inputs used

The inputs are those in [the worked example](example.md), under "The inputs": the facts typed by the head of partnerships, Partner A's application form and cover email, the meeting notes of 4 and 6 November 2026, the six-row accounts export with the default headers, and the economics from the finance director. One line was added to the meeting notes of 6 November after the first run, so the first run did not have it: "Both Account G and Account H are property managers with more than 20 buildings each." The inputs have not changed since the second run. The review date is 9 November 2026, as the inputs give it; the template says never to assume today's date.

## How the run was made

The instruction block and output format as revised after the second review, and the example's inputs, were put together, and the review was worked through from them step by step, in the order the instruction block gives. The run was made by the model that made the second set of revisions, in the same session, after it had read the second run's record, the review's points and the published example. It is therefore not independent: it shows that the revised instructions can be followed to the end and give the revised example, not how a fresh reader would take them. The output below was then compared line by line with the published example and with the second run's output.

## What was observed

**Every required part was produced.** All sections of the output format are present, in order, with one blank decision line and the closing sentence. Nothing told the run to stop: the export headers matched, the review date was given, and the material was about one partner.

**It matches the published example.** The output above and the example's output agree line for line. Because the same model revised both in the same session, that agreement is expected and is not evidence that a fresh reader would produce the same review.

**What differs from the second run.** Only the lines for Accounts J and K. Each now ends "not confirmed: call not reached, 6 November 2026", which the output format now provides for, so the unreached calls in the notes of 6 November are no longer dropped. Three other places the second run had to judge now follow written rules and came out the same as before: the From line's "economics supplied for referral and reseller (alliance not offered)" now fits the format, which says how to print a type the organization does not offer; the alliance requirement "people or money committed in writing" is met by the sales director's written commitment alone, as the template now says one named person committed in writing is enough without a set minimum; and the sales director is the partner's contact in the plan because the sales director's cover email says "I will lead this partnership on our side", which the template now says makes that person the named contact.

**The figures recompute.** Direct margin \$12,000 (50.0%). Referral: fixed cost \$2,400 plus the \$6,000 asked = \$8,400; per-deal break-even (7,200 − 4,800) ÷ 24,000 = 10.0%; year break-even (7,200 − 4,800 − 840) ÷ 24,000 = 6.5%, \$1,560 a deal. Reseller: fixed cost \$15,000; per-deal break-even 25.0%; year break-even (7,200 − 1,200 − 1,500) ÷ 24,000 = 18.75%, printed 18.8%; at 30%, margin 24,000 − 4,800 − 7,200 − 1,200 = \$10,800 (45.0%), \$1,200 less than direct, and 7,200 − 1,200 − 7,200 is below zero, so never at this rate. The gap is 30.0 − 18.8 = 11.2 points. Alliance is not offered, so it has no economics. Pace 3 deals and 12 qualified opportunities a quarter; signs 4, 6 and 12 working, 3 and 6 ending; closed deals first judged at day 30 + 75 = 105; 30 November is 26 days after 4 November.

**Where a fresh reader could still have to guess.**
1. The alliance requirement of a one-sentence combined offering with the customer it serves was marked "stated only": the partner gave the sentence but named no customer. The template does not say whether a requirement the partner states in part is "stated only" or "not met". It does not change the result, because the alliance also lacks senior sponsors.
2. The referral result still rests on the line added to the notes of 6 November after the first run. Without it, the rule would mark referral "not met: market not recorded", support no type, write no plan, and ask what kind of organizations Accounts G and H are.
3. With seven questions the cap of ten was not reached, so how a run leaves questions out was still not seen.

**Facts not in the inputs.** None was found in the output.

**What this run does not show.** The template's acceptance tests are proposed and none was run. One run by the model that revised the template, on one invented example, shows that the revised instructions can be followed to the end and give the published example. It does not show how a fresh reader would take them, and it does not show the template works for a real partner. A run on these instructions by a model that did not revise them has not been made.

## The second run, and what was changed after it

The second run, on 10 November 2026 in Claude Code, was made with Claude Opus 5.5 (model ID `claude-opus-5-5`), the model that made the first revision, in the same session, on the instructions as revised after the first review and the inputs above. Its output agreed line for line with the example as then published, and with the output above except for the lines for Accounts J and K, which read:

5. Account J, service contract, September 2026: new to you; stated by the partner.
6. Account K, building-management system install, October 2026: new to you; stated by the partner.

Its observations, as recorded, were these. "The output above" in them means its own output.

**Every required part was produced.** All sections of the output format are present, in order, with one blank decision line and the closing sentence. Nothing told the run to stop: the export headers matched, the review date was given, and the material was about one partner.

**It matches the published example.** The output above and the example's output agree line for line. Because the same model wrote both in the same session, that agreement is expected and is not evidence that a fresh reader would produce the same review.

**The figures recompute.** Direct margin \$12,000 (50.0%). Referral: fixed cost \$2,400 plus the \$6,000 asked = \$8,400; per-deal break-even (7,200 − 4,800) ÷ 24,000 = 10.0%; year break-even (7,200 − 4,800 − 840) ÷ 24,000 = 6.5%, \$1,560 a deal. Reseller: fixed cost \$15,000; per-deal break-even 25.0%; year break-even (7,200 − 1,200 − 1,500) ÷ 24,000 = 18.75%, printed 18.8%; at 30%, margin \$10,800 (45.0%), \$1,200 less than direct, and 7,200 − 1,200 − 7,200 is below zero, so never at this rate. The gap is 30.0 − 18.8 = 11.2 points, from the printed figures as the revised step says. Pace 3 deals and 12 qualified opportunities a quarter; signs 4, 6 and 12 working, 3 and 6 ending; closed deals first judged at day 30 + 75 = 105; 30 November is 26 days after 4 November.

**Where a fresh reader could still have to guess.**
1. The evidence line has no place to say a customer could not be reached. The run marked Accounts J and K "stated by the partner", as the revised rule says, and left out that the calls of 6 November did not reach them.
2. The "From" line offers economics "supplied" or "missing". The run wrote "supplied for referral and reseller (alliance not offered)": a type the organization does not offer is not a missing figure, but the format does not say so.
3. The referral result rests on the line added to the notes of 6 November. On the inputs the earlier run had, the revised rule would mark referral "not met: market not recorded", support no type, write no plan, and ask what kind of organizations Accounts G and H are.
4. For an alliance, the run counted the sales director's written commitment to lead the partnership as "people committed in writing". The template does not say whether one person is enough; no minimum was set, so one confirmed item met it.
5. The cap of ten questions and the order of questions were followed, but with seven questions the cap was not reached, so how a run leaves questions out was not seen.

**Facts not in the inputs.** None was found in the output.

**What this run does not show.** The template's acceptance tests are proposed and none was run. One run by the model that revised the template, on one invented example, shows that the revised instructions can be followed to the end and give the published example. It does not show the template works for a real partner.

**What was changed after it.** A second independent review raised eight minor points. In response:
1. The output format's evidence line gained "not confirmed: attempt, date" for a claim stated by the partner that an attempt failed to confirm, and the procedure and the pasted instructions say to record it. The example's lines for Accounts J and K now carry it.
2. The output format's From line now says how to print a type the organization does not offer, and the procedure and the pasted instructions say such a type is not a missing figure and is left out of the economics.
3. The procedure and the pasted instructions now say that a commitment in writing by the partner meets a requirement of a written commitment, that one named person is enough without a set minimum, for an alliance as for a reseller, and that a commitment only said in a meeting is "stated only".
4. The procedure and the pasted instructions now say that the person the partner's material names as leading the partnership on its side is the partner's named contact.
5. The pasted instructions' warning sign "no customer named" now reads as the procedure does, a customer that bought from the partner in the past year, and they now carry the procedure's rule that a name that cannot be checked against the export is "not checked".
6. The public page now says the review runs before anyone promises a type, a rate or a region, as the template does, and the source credits now name the idea of a combined offering in one sentence with the customer it serves.
Nothing in the example's inputs changed.

## The first run, and what was changed after it

The first run, on 10 November 2026 in Claude Code, was made with Claude Sonnet 5.5 (model ID `claude-sonnet-5-5`), a model that did not write the template, on the template as first written and the inputs without the line on the market of Accounts G and H. It followed the instruction block and output format, and did not read the example's output until its own was finished. Its full output is not reproduced here. Its observations, as recorded, were these; only the headings were put in the past tense.

**Every required part was produced.** All sections of the output format are present, in order, with one blank decision line and the closing sentence. Nothing in the instructions told the assistant to stop, because the inputs were complete: the export headers matched, the review date was given, and there was material about one partner.

**Facts not in the inputs.** None was found in this run's output. In the published example, nothing was found that the inputs do not support either.

**Where it matched the example then published.** Customer marks (2 already in records, 4 new, 2 of those confirmed), the five warning signs, referral as the supported type with reseller asked, every reseller figure (break-even 25.0% and 18.8%, margin $10,800 at 30%, "never at this rate", 11.2 points above), and the whole plan: 3 deals and 12 qualified opportunities a quarter, targets of 4, 6 and 12, ending signs of 3 and 6, first judgement of closed deals at day 105, and the agreement term set aside for the chief operating officer.

**Differences from the example then published.**
- Referral year break-even: this run gave 6.5% ($1,560 a deal), the example 9.0% ($2,160). The template says the fixed cost F is the type's fixed yearly cost "plus any marketing money the partner asks for". This run added the $6,000 to the referral type too, giving $8,400; the example added it only to the reseller type. The template does not say whether marketing money asked under a reseller request applies to a referral. Both readings are defensible, and the difference changes the referral range (below 6.5% or below 9.0%). The example's own plan treats the $6,000 as still on the table for referral, which fits this run's reading better.
- This run counted the webinar offer under "Other" asks; the example wrote "none".
- This run wrote nine questions, the example six. The three extra: the reason for the 30 November date, whether the two customers' market is one you sell to, and whether to answer by 30 November.
- The example's question 5 also asks what the partner must achieve to keep an exclusive region; this run did not, as that edges toward proposing terms.
- Small wording: this run quoted the agreement term without "we would want", and wrote the two accounts' since dates in year-month-day form where the example spells them out.

**Where the instructions were ambiguous and a guess was needed.**
1. Marketing money for a type other than the one asked (above).
2. Rounding. Year break-even for reseller is exactly 18.75%, which rounds to 18.8%. The gap to the 30% ask is 11.25 points exactly, which would round to 11.3, but 11.2 is what results from the printed figures. The template does not say which to use. This run used 11.2, matching the template's own acceptance test 5.
3. "In a market you sell to" (the referral requirement). The inputs do not say what kind of organization Accounts G and H are. This run marked the requirement met on the confirmed new customers and added a question. A stricter reading would mark it "stated only" and leave no supported type.
4. "Stated by the partner" versus "unknown". Accounts J and K were named by the partner and could not be reached. This run used "stated by the partner" and noted the unreached calls. The template does not say when "unknown" applies.
5. "The first meeting". The 4 November call was taken as the first; the notes do not say so. The 26 days to 30 November rests on that.
6. How people whose job is selling can be confirmed. The template says a document from someone other than the partner or a conversation with the customer; it is unclear what that would be for sellers. The what-would-change-the-fit line is therefore loose.
7. Whether to run a type the partner did not ask for and the evidence does not support (alliance) through the full test. This run did, as step 4 says "each type".
8. Whether the partner's contact should be a role from the inputs. The cover email gives the sales director as the lead; this run took that as the partner's named contact.

**Shortcomings it found in use.**
- The output format is long and the questions step produces more items than the example shows; there is no cap or ordering rule for them.
- Several steps are applied to a type the partner did not ask for (referral economics) with no rule on which of the partner's asks carry over.
- The template's own acceptance tests say they are proposed and have not been run; this trial ran none of them.
- One run on one example by one assistant shows the instructions can be followed to the end. It does not show the template works for a real partner.

**What was changed after it.**
1. Marketing money. The procedure and the pasted instructions now say that marketing money asked is added to the fixed cost of every type worked out, whichever type it was asked with. The example was corrected to follow that rule, which the template already stated: the referral fixed cost is now \$8,400, its year break-even 6.5% (\$1,560 a deal) and its range below 6.5%, the figures the first run produced. An acceptance test for this case was added.
2. Rounding. The gap between the ask and the range is now the difference between the two printed percentages, in the procedure, the pasted instructions and acceptance test 5, so 11.2 stands.
3. Market. The referral requirement now says the market is judged from what the notes or the partner's material record about the customer, against the rule for a qualified opportunity, and that a customer whose kind of organization is not recorded does not count. The pasted instructions now carry the market condition, which they had dropped. The example's notes of 6 November gained the line recording that Accounts G and H are property managers with more than 20 buildings each, and an acceptance test for an unrecorded market was added.
4. "Unknown". The procedure and the pasted instructions now say "stated by the partner" covers a claim nothing confirms, including a customer who could not be reached, and "unknown" applies when the inputs do not make clear the partner claims it at all.
5. First meeting. Both now say the first meeting is the earliest dated conversation with the partner in the notes. The example's warning sign now names the call of 4 November as the first meeting.
6. Sellers. Both now say sellers are confirmed only by a named customer saying, in a conversation with you, that it bought through one of them, or by a document from someone other than the partner naming them in a selling role; names and roles from the partner stay "stated by the partner". The example's "What would change the fit" line and its first question were rewritten to match.
7. Questions. The questions now come in a fixed order, with items of the same kind for the same role in one question, nothing that suggests a rate, a term or a condition, and at most ten, saying how many were left out; the output format gained a line for that. The example now has seven questions: it gained one asking what the 30 November date rests on, and its question on the exclusive region was trimmed to the choice alone. The founder's question on whether to answer by 30 November, which the first run asked, is not among the kinds listed, so the example does not ask it.
8. Offers. Something the partner offers rather than asks, such as co-hosting an event, is now a commitment, not an ask, so "Other: none" stands and the webinars stay under Commitments.
9. The pasted instructions now carry "not checked: accounts export not supplied", which the procedure and output format already used.
10. The example's "Region held alone" now reads "Region or segment held alone", as the output format has it, and its marketing-money step in the plan no longer adds "such as the two webinars offered", which the instructions do not ask for.
