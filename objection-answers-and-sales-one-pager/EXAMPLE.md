# Objection answers and sales one-pager: an invented example

*Illustrative example with invented data.* Quillhaven Fleet Care, Brambleside Provisions and everyone in this example are invented. Quillhaven is a 35-person company that maintains delivery-van fleets. Dana, its account executive, has a second call with Brambleside on Thursday 12 November 2026 and prepares for it on Monday 9 November.

The inputs come first, complete, exactly as Dana supplied them. The call pack follows. It is what the template returned when it was tried on these inputs, set out here with headings. Two short passages were taken out afterwards, and the trial record published with this template quotes both. The record also lists the smaller places where the pack departs from the instructions; those are left in here as they came out.

### Objection answers

**1. Objection:** "We run Saturdays. What happens when a van fails on a Saturday?" (operations manager, first call, 3 November 2026)

- **Kind:** fit. A question: "What happens when...?"
- **The worry behind it:** either a van stranded on a route day with nobody to come out, or how long a failed van stays off the road. Evidence: "We run Saturdays" (call notes) points to the first; the second is a guess.
- **Ask first:** "When a van fails on a route, which matters more to you: someone coming out to it that day, or how soon it is back on the road?"
- **Say:** "You're right to press on this, because you told us routes run Monday to Saturday. Our technicians work 9pm to 5am, Monday night to Friday night. There is no weekend service and no daytime breakdown call-out, so we would not come out to a van on a Saturday. For repairs outside the schedule, parts are billed at cost plus 15% and labour is included in the plan. What I can't tell you today is how soon a failed van gets into a night window, and I'll find that out for you. Who comes out to a van that fails on a route today?"
- **Limit:** no weekend service and no daytime call-out on any day, not only Saturday (F6). The facts do not say how or how soon a failed van is booked for repair (F3 covers price only).
- **Proof to have ready:** \[needs: how a failed van is booked into a night window and how soon, from the operations lead\]
- **Ask next:** "How often did a van fail on a route in the last year?"
- **Stop arguing when:** they need the servicing supplier to attend breakdowns in the daytime or on Saturdays. Then say: "We don't offer that, and I'd rather tell you now than later."

**2. Objection:** "Can you look after the fridge units as well?" (operations manager, first call, 3 November 2026)

- **Kind:** fit. A question: "Can you...?"
- **The worry behind it:** wanting one supplier for the van and the unit rather than managing two. Evidence: "as well" (call notes).
- **Say:** "It's a fair thing to ask, since six of your vans carry units. The answer is no: we don't service refrigeration units. A van that carries one is taken on the plan and we service the van; the unit itself is not serviced. Bodywork and tyres are not offered either, so you have the whole list. Would keeping your present arrangement for the units work for you?"
- **Limit:** refrigeration units, bodywork and tyres are not offered (F7).
- **Proof to have ready:** none needed; F7 (fact sheet, 2 November 2026) is the answer.
- **Ask next:** "Who services the six units today, and is it on the same visit as the van?"
- **Stop arguing when:** they need one supplier for van and unit together. Then say: "We can't be that supplier for the units. We'd take the six vans, not their units."

**3. Objection:** "The dealer quotes us about \$140 a van. You're a third more expensive. I'd need a reason." (finance director, email, 5 November 2026)

- **Kind:** price or value. Wants a reason: "I'd need a reason."
- **The worry behind it:** paying a higher per-van price without seeing what it buys. Evidence: "You're a third more expensive. I'd need a reason." (email)
- **Say:** "You're right to want a reason before paying a different price, and I haven't seen the dealer's quote, so I won't set the two figures against each other. Our price is \$189 per van per month for scheduled servicing and safety inspections, done overnight in your own yard by our technicians. Your operations manager told us, 'We lose a van for a day and a half every time one goes in'; a van we service overnight is ready to drive by 5am. For July to September 2026 our service log shows 1,412 scheduled services booked and 1,377 completed in the booked night window; that is our own record, not audited. I can't put a dollar figure on a van off the road for you; that number is yours. What does the dealer's quote cover, and over what period?"
- **Limit:**
  - We hold no figure for what a van off the road costs a customer and no comparison with dealer workshop prices (Not held).
  - The facts do not state that our service removes their day and a half. The two statements stand side by side only.
  - The only discount listed is 5% for fleets of 100 vans or more (F4). The notes record 60 vans.
  - Parts for repairs outside the schedule are extra, at cost plus 15% (F3).
- **Proof to have ready:** service log, July to September 2026, F8, approved for sharing by the operations lead on 2 October 2026.
- **Ask next:** "How many times a year does each van go into the dealer's workshop?"
- **Stop arguing when:** the finance director says the per-van price alone decides. Then say: "Our price is \$189 per van per month, and the only discount we list is for fleets of 100 vans or more." A request for a different price goes to the sales lead. A question on agreement terms goes to the managing director.

**4. Objection:** "Who else like us uses you?" (Expected, not yet heard)

- **Kind:** proof and trust.
- **The worry behind it:** either whether we have done this for a fleet like theirs, or whether anyone outside our company can vouch for us. A guess.
- **Ask first:** "When you say 'like us', what matters most: the size of the fleet, the kind of goods, or the Monday-to-Saturday routes?"
- **Say:** "That's the right thing to check, and I'd rather you heard it from a customer than from me. \[Held: "One of our customers, a regional florist running 45 vans, has agreed to take reference calls." Released by: sales lead. If not released, say: "One of our customers has agreed to take reference calls; I need my sales lead's approval before I say more about them."\] I can't tell you they are like you; you'd be the judge of that, and we hold no written customer quotes to hand over. I can ask for the introduction after this call. What would you want to ask them?"
- **Limit:** one reference only, with the name released per request by the sales lead (F9). The facts hold no bakery-supply or wholesale customer, no customer list and no written quotes.
- **Proof to have ready:** the reference customer, F9, permission recorded 14 August 2026, held for the sales lead. If they want figures instead, the F8 service log is the other shareable item; bring one at a time.
- **Ask next:** "How did you check the dealer before you first used them?"
- **Stop arguing when:** they need a reference from a business of their own kind or size. Then say: "I don't have one to offer."

**5. Objection:** "We'd rather wait until the new year." (Expected, not yet heard)

- **Kind:** timing.
- **The worry behind it:** it could be budget timing, a busy period before year end, an arrangement with the dealer that runs to a date, or a polite no. A guess.
- **Ask first:** "What changes for you in the new year?"
- **Say:** "That's reasonable, and nothing I have says you need to decide sooner. If it helps you decide, we offer a free two-week yard assessment covering up to 10 vans, and it carries no obligation to take the plan. It's booked through our service coordinator, so I can't give you dates on this call, and I don't hold start dates for new fleets either. Would you like me to ask about the assessment, or shall we simply talk again in the new year?"
- **Limit:** no start dates for new fleets (Not held) and no assessment dates in F10. The 12-month minimum (F4) is a term, so any question on it goes to the managing director.
- **Proof to have ready:** assessment offer, F10, fact sheet 2 November 2026. \[needs: assessment availability, from the service coordinator\]
- **Ask next:** "What does the fleet's workload look like between now and the new year?"
- **Stop arguing when:** they say they will wait, because the facts hold no reason to press. Then say: "Understood. When would you like me to get back in touch?"
