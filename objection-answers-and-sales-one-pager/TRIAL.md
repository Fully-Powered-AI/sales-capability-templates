# Objection answers and sales one-pager: one trial on its invented example

- **Product:** Claude, run in Claude Code
- **Model:** Claude Fable 5.2
- **Date:** 2026-11-09
- **Template version:** 0.1.0
- **Runs:** the run recorded here is the last of three made the same day. The two before it, and what each changed, are summarised at the end.

This is a record of one run. The inputs are the invented example published with this capability template: Quillhaven Fleet Care, Brambleside Provisions, Dana and every fact, figure and quotation in them are invented. No client data was used, nothing was sent, and no one was contacted. One run on an invented example shows how the instructions behave once; it is not a test in anyone's working environment.

## How the run was made

The instruction block from the template was given as the custom instructions, followed by the output format, as the template directs. The bracketed settings in the block were replaced with the values Dana gives in her request (proof rechecked after six months; a 250-word one-pager; prices confirmed by the sales lead; terms questions to the managing director; the "Calls" tab of the sales sheet as the objection log), as the template also directs.

The three input blocks of [the worked example](example.md) were then pasted in full as one message: Quillhaven's fact sheet of 2 November 2026 (facts F1 to F11 and a "Not held" line), Dana's notes of the first call on 3 November with the finance director's email of 5 November, and the two expected objections with the request.

The run was a fresh session with no tools, no files and no project instructions. It saw the block, the format and the inputs, and nothing else: not the rest of the template, not the earlier runs, not any earlier example. The session that revised the template started the run and wrote this record.

## What was observed

**Every part of the output format was produced.** The header and status line, six notes on the inputs, a quick sheet of five lines, five objection entries, a one-pager with its eight parts and a word count, open items for the buyer and four roles, a fact check of 28 rows, the closing line and the after-the-call line. Every "Say" ends in a question, every entry has a limit, and each quick-sheet line carries its limit.

**Nothing told the run to stop.** A dated, owned fact sheet was present. The notes and the fact sheet did not disagree, no question about the terms of an agreement was raised by the buyer, and nobody asked for a figure, customer or deadline the facts do not hold. Those stop conditions were therefore not exercised, and neither was the short form for a single objection. Dana's log had no entries, so the line about past objections was not exercised either. No objection ended as "No supported answer".

**What the run did that the instructions ask for.**

- It found the three objections heard inside the notes and the email, although Dana listed only the two she expects, and said so in the notes on the inputs. It put them in date order and treated the two questions as heard because the truthful reply to each is no.
- It marked what the buyer wants on the three heard entries ("A question", "A question", "Wants a reason") and left that out on the two expected ones.
- The dealer's figure appears only inside the buyer's quotation. The pack does not repeat it in its own voice, compares nothing against it and calculates nothing. The buyer's "a third more" is labelled as the buyer's own sum.
- It joined no facts into a conclusion in what is said to the buyer. "We lose a van for a day and a half" and "a van we service overnight is ready to drive by 5am" stand side by side in answer 3, and the limit says the facts do not state that one removes the other. The conclusions it was tempted by are under open items as lines someone could add to the facts.
- Both gaps (Saturday failures and refrigeration units) are answered with a plain no and a stop condition.
- The reference is written as a held line in answer 4 and on the one-pager, each with who releases it and what to say if it is not released. Answer 4 brings the reference only; the service log is named under "Proof to have ready" with "bring one at a time".
- Where more than one worry fitted (entries 1, 4 and 5) it named each and wrote an "Ask first" question that differs from the question closing the answer. Where the buyer had said nothing, it wrote "A guess".
- The 100-van discount is left off the one-pager and kept in the limit of answer 3.
- The one-pager's count was checked by counting: 229 words from headline to contact with the held line left out, as stated; 257 with it. The limit was 250.
- The fact check uses the five labels as defined.

**Where the output departs from the instructions or the format.**

1. Entry 3 carries an extra line that the format has no place for: "The dealer figure appears only as the buyer's quotation: Buyer's report, not checked. Do not repeat it as fact or compare against it." It is an instruction echoed back, not part of an entry.
2. The stop line of entry 4 ends: "The one reference I may be able to arrange is a different kind of business." The facts describe the reference as a regional florist and the notes describe the buyer as a bakery-supply wholesaler; "a different kind of business" is a conclusion joined from the two, which the rules say not to state. It also describes a customer outside a held line.
3. "So we would not come out to a van on a Saturday" in answer 1 restates "no weekend service" in the buyer's terms. It is close to the fact, but it is not the fact as written.
4. The "Kind" lines shorten the buyer's question to "What happens when...?" and "Can you...?" where the instructions ask for the words that show it.
5. The quick-sheet lines for the two gaps read "Proof: none held (F6, F3)" and "none held (F7)", which mixes "no proof" with a fact reference.
6. Fact-check row 28 (the finance director was not on the first call) is not used in anything said to the buyer; row 9 labels the buyer's own sum as a report about another supplier, which stretches that label.
7. The header says "account executive (taking the call)" and not Dana's name, although the request and F11 name her.

**Shortcomings a reviewer would act on.** Answers 1 and 3 are six long sentences each; a salesperson would shorten them before a call. Answer 1 promises "I'll find that out for you", which is the salesperson's promise to make, not the pack's. The open items run to four roles and nine missing facts, and one of them ("Nothing to answer yet", for the managing director) asks nothing. Keeping strictly to the facts as written has a cost that shows here: the pack will not say that a yard 22 miles from a depot is inside a 40-mile service area, and offers that sentence to the sales lead as a line to add. The one-pager's headline is plain. The pack needs the salesperson's edit and the sales lead's review before any of it is said or sent, which is what the template says will happen.

**Against the template's review checklist.** On one reading by the session that wrote this record: the rows marked Copied agree with the fact sheet in every figure, but rows 4, 7, 16, 18 and 23 shorten or reword the fact, so "Copied" is loose there and a reviewer checking word for word would mark them; the three objections heard are quoted exactly and attributed; every answer has its limit; prices and terms are copied and nothing is calculated; the reference is held wherever it appears, apart from the sentence noted in point 2; both gaps are named as gaps; the one-pager is inside the limit and says nothing the fact check does not list; nothing was sent. The checklist is for the sales lead, the salesperson and the pricing owner to work through; this reading is not their review.

## The two earlier runs

**A run on the first draft of the template.** It used the first draft's example inputs and was made in the same product with the same model. It produced a complete pack and recorded fourteen places where the instructions had to be guessed at, and it showed that the example then published said things its own fact sheet did not hold. The template and the example were revised in response:

- the block now carries what it lacked: what to do when notes and the fact sheet disagree, leaving out personal details, marking a worry as a guess, the optional log of past objections and the after-the-call reminder;
- the proof age limit has a default of 12 months;
- pushback inside the notes and emails counts as heard, and so does a question whose truthful reply may be no;
- what the buyer wants is marked on objections heard only;
- joining two facts into a conclusion is ruled out, with a place under open items for conclusions worth adding to the facts;
- a reference, a record and a figure each count as one proof item;
- a held line has a written form that includes what to say if it is not released;
- the fact check is kept as the pack is written, with five labels in place of three;
- the format has a place for notes on the inputs, and says what the word count covers;
- conditional discounts stay off the one-pager;
- items for a role nobody named are addressed to the role in plain words;
- the example's fact sheet gained three statements the old example had relied on without holding them: that a van serviced overnight is ready to drive by 5am, that a van carrying a refrigeration unit is taken on the plan, and that the assessment carries no obligation. Dana's request now gives her settings.

**A first run on the revised template.** Made the same way as the run recorded here. It followed the new rules, and showed three further gaps: for an expected objection it wrote "A guess" and named no worry and no "Ask first" question; its notes on the inputs ran to eleven items; and its one-pager listed, under "What is not included", the figures the fact sheet does not hold. The block was changed on those three points only (a guess still names the worry, and more than one worry gets an "Ask first" question that differs from the closing question; the notes are kept to six short lines; the "not held" list stays out of the one-pager). The run recorded above was then made on the block as it now stands.
