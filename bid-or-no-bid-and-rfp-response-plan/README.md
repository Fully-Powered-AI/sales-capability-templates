# Bid or no-bid and RFP response plan

Before anyone starts on a response, your bid manager sees every requirement in the request checked against your own evidence, your bid criteria tested and the buyer questions ready. A named person decides whether to bid; then a dated plan says who does what by when.

*Capability template · Sales · version 0.1.0 · [Capabilities Directory](https://fullypowered.ai/capabilities/bid-or-no-bid-and-rfp-response-plan/)*

## The outcome

The decision to bid is made on the first day or two, on facts, by the person whose call it is. They see the request as a list: what the buyer insists on, what earns points, what is merely welcome, and which items your own licences, certificates, case summaries and references actually prove. A must-have you cannot meet shows up before anyone spends a week writing, not the night before the deadline.

Your bid criteria, written before the request arrived, are tested one by one as met, not met or unknown, with the fact behind each. There is no made-up chance of winning; the decision-maker weighs the facts and decides.

Once they decide to bid, the team gets a plan counted back from the deadline in your own working days: every section with its owner, reviewer and draft date, the sections worth the most points started first, and the conditions the decision-maker set turned into dated actions. Your people still write the answers, set the price and review the buyer's terms. The AI assistant decides nothing, sends nothing and submits nothing.

## Who starts the work, and when

The bid manager, proposal lead or owner starts it the day a request for proposals, a tender or a request for quotation arrives, well before the buyer's deadline for questions. The decision pack goes to the person your bid criteria name as the decision-maker, usually the owner or managing director.

When that person has recorded the decision, the bid manager runs the second part to get the plan, and runs it again when the buyer's answers arrive, someone is away or a condition changes the dates. The bid manager approves the plan before the team sees it.

## What they supply

- **The buyer's request,** in full, with the attachments you have.
- **Your evidence list.** One row per licence, certificate, insurance certificate, case summary, reference or policy, with what it proves, when it expires and who owns it.
- **Your bid criteria,** and who decides. If you have none, the capability starts from a standard set and leaves the limits for you to fill in.
- **Deal notes.** How you heard of the request, whether you spoke with the buyer before it was issued, who holds the work today, how many rivals you expect, and whether you can staff it.
- **For the plan:** the decision as recorded, who writes and reviews each section, who approves the price and the final response, who submits, and who is away.

## What comes out

**A decision pack.** Every requirement, in the order the request lists it, with its kind and how well your evidence covers it, the must-haves you do not fully meet, the points at risk, evidence that is out of date, each bid criterion with its result, draft questions for the buyer dated to the buyer's deadline, and the decision laid out with the date it is needed by.

**Then a response plan,** or a short no-bid record. The plan holds every milestone and section with its owner and date, the actions the decision-maker set as conditions, who handles the price and the terms, and each problem (a missing owner, someone away on their due date, a person reviewing their own section) written as a question for the bid manager.

A good pack can be read and decided on in one sitting, and a good plan surprises nobody whose name is on it.

## What has to change for your company

Write your bid criteria and their limits before the next request arrives, and name who decides. Build the evidence list once, with an owner for each item and its expiry date, and keep it current. Decide your lead times: the defaults suit a response of a few weeks, from section drafts nine working days before the deadline to submission the working day before it. Name who sets and approves prices, who reviews a buyer's terms, who gives final approval and who submits. Agree where the approved plan lives. It runs in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini, from files and notes you supply; it needs no access to a buyer's portal.

## Start using it

1. Read [EXAMPLE.md](EXAMPLE.md) to see what a good result looks like.
2. Open [TEMPLATE.md](TEMPLATE.md) and set every item under "What to customize" to your own rules, roles and limits.
3. Give your AI assistant the instruction block and the output format from the template, with your own limits in place of the ones the instructions leave open in square brackets. Paste them in as custom instructions in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini, or copy this whole folder into a product that loads `SKILL.md` packages. This folder stands alone; you do not need the rest of the repository.
4. Run it on past work first, using the reviewer checklist and acceptance tests in the template, before anyone relies on it.
